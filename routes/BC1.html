    Concept

    DC-DC Converters are power electronics circuits that convert a dc input voltage to a different dc output voltage level.

    Learning Objective

    1. Characteristics of buck converter
    2. Capacitor and inductor selection
    3. Output voltage and current
    4. Continuous and discontinuous mode

    Materials and Equipment

    Power supply, capacitor, inductor, transistor, diode, resistor

    Procedure

    1. Make connections of power supply, capacitor, inductor, transistor and load resistor to build the buck converter.
      Vin = 50 V, L = 400 μH, C = 100 μF, f = 20 kHz, R = 20 Ω
    2. Check the circuit connections according to the schematics.
    3. Varying duty cycle
    4. Set the duty cycle at 40%, and switching frequency at 20 kHz.
    5. Observe and measure the output voltage.
    6. Vary the duty cycle from 20% to 80% with the interval of 10%.
    7. Measure the output voltage for each of the duty cycle. Compare the measurement with theoretical calculation.
    8. Varying switching frequency
      1. Set the duty cycle at 40%, and switching frequency at 20 kHz.
      2. Observe and measure the output voltage ripple. Compare the measurement with theoretical calculation.
      3. Increase the switching frequency to 50 kHz. Observe and measure the output voltage ripple. Compare the measurement with theoretical calculation.
      4. Decrease the switching frequency to 10 kHz. Observe and measure the output voltage ripple. Compare the measurement with theoretical calculation.
    9. Varying load resistor
      1. Set the duty cycle at 40%, and switching frequency at 20 kHz.
      2. Measure the load current and output voltage when the load resistance is 20 Ω.
      3. Measure the load current and output voltage when the load resistance is 40 Ω.
